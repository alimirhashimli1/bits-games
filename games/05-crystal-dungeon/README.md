# 💎 Game 05: Crystal Dungeon

A top-down action adventure inspired by **The Legend of Zelda** (1986). Explore an open overworld one screen at a time, talk to villagers, find keys and treasure, clear three dungeons and defeat the Crystal Guardian. All characters, places, items and music are original.

## Story

For three hundred years the **Heartcrystal** has sat at the top of the **Crystal Spire**, keeping the valley of **Glimmervale** green and its nights short. Its keeper, the **Crystal Guardian**, was built to protect it. One night the Guardian turned the Heartcrystal's light inward, the crystal cracked, and two of its shards fell into the valley. Monsters crawled out of the cracks after them. **Wren**, the lamplighter's apprentice from the village of **Emberfen**, is the only one who saw where the shards fell. With her grandmother's old sword she sets out to bring both shards back, climb the Spire and set the Guardian right.

## How it plays

- **Explore.** The overworld is a grid of screens. Walking off the edge of one scrolls the view to the next. Caves, houses and dungeon doors lead to rooms of their own.
- **Fight.** Wren moves in eight directions and faces four. A sword swing hits in front of her. At full health the swing also throws a **spark** that flies across the screen. Enemies are knocked back when hit, and so is Wren, who then flashes and cannot be hurt for a moment.
- **Shield.** While Wren is walking or standing still, her shield blocks small projectiles (pebbles, darts) coming at her face.
- **Hearts.** Wren starts with three hearts, counted in halves. Each boss leaves a **heart container**, and two more are hidden in the overworld. Defeated enemies sometimes drop a heart.
- **Gems.** Enemies and hidden spots drop gems (worth 1, 5 or 20). Gems buy things at the Emberfen shop: a stronger shield, bomb refills and a potion that refills every heart.
- **Items** (all original), found one in each of the first two dungeons and one in the overworld:
  - **Moonrang**: a curved blade that flies out and back. It stuns enemies and brings back gems, hearts and keys it touches.
  - **Bombs**: blow open cracked walls and hurt everything nearby. Found as a bomb bag, refilled from drops and the shop.
  - **Lantern**: lights dark rooms and the torches in them. Given by the old hermit in the eastern hills.
- **Dungeons.** Every dungeon is a grid of rooms. Doors can be open, **locked** (a small key opens one), **shut** until every enemy in the room is defeated, or **cracked** (bombs). A dungeon also holds its **map**, which shows every room on the pause screen, its **compass**, which marks the boss, and a **boss key** for the boss door.
- **Puzzles.** Push blocks, pressure plates, switches, dark rooms, spike traps that slide at Wren when she lines up with them, and torches to light.
- **Enemies** (all original):
  - **Blub**: a jelly that hops towards Wren. One hit.
  - **Pebblenose**: walks in straight lines and spits pebbles the way it faces.
  - **Flitter**: a bat that flies in loops and rests now and then.
  - **Thornback**: an armoured boar that charges when Wren is in line with it.
  - **Rattler**: a skeleton that sidesteps and jumps back from the sword.
  - **Ironhelm**: a knight with a tower shield. Only his sides and back can be hit.
  - **Wisp**: a ghost that drifts through walls. Only a stunned Wisp can be hurt.
  - **Shardling**: a small crystal that splits into two smaller ones when hit.
  - **Spikebox**: a trap that cannot be defeated.
- **Three dungeons and three bosses:**
  1. **Mossroot Cellar** under the forest. Treasure: the Moonrang. Boss: **Bogmaw**, a giant toad that lashes its tongue and leaps. Stun it with the Moonrang, then strike.
  2. **Sunken Vault** under the lake. Treasure: the bomb bag. Boss: **Stonefist**, a golem whose armour cracks under bombs.
  3. **Crystal Spire** on the mountain, opened by the two shards. It needs every item, and its dark rooms need the Lantern. Boss: the **Crystal Guardian**, fought in three phases.
- **Saving.** The game saves when Wren enters a dungeon, finishes one, or chooses *Save and continue* on the Game Over screen. Continuing starts at the village or the last dungeon entrance with full hearts.
- **Ending.** The Heartcrystal is whole again, the valley lights up, and the credits show how long the adventure took and how many times Wren fell.

## Technical plan

- **Engine:** Phaser with **Arcade physics**, top-down, so no gravity. Bodies are smaller than their sprites (the feet and lower body only), so Wren's head and shoulders can overlap the tile above her, as if she stood in front of it.
- **Resolution:** 320×180 through `@shared/phaser/createPixelGame`. A 20 px HUD sits at the top and every screen or room below it is **20×10 tiles** of 16 px (320×160).
- **Rooms as text maps** (`src/content/world/`, `src/content/dungeons/`): each overworld screen and dungeon room is a 20×10 text map, one character per tile, with a legend of frames and solid tiles, loaded into a Phaser tilemap as in game 02. The overworld is a grid of screen maps, and each dungeon a grid of rooms with the door on each side named in the room data.
  - **Loader** (`src/systems/roomLoader.ts`, legend in `src/content/world/tileLegend.ts`): checks that a map is exactly 20×10 and uses only known characters, naming the room, row and column in any error, then builds the tilemap. Game 02's loader was mostly about pipes, blocks and its boss, so it did not move to `shared/`; the part both games use (tilemap, tileset, the check for a real `TilemapLayer`, collision) is now `@shared/phaser/tileLayer`, and game 02 uses it too.
  - **Overworld tiles** (`src/content/sprites/tiles.ts`): grass, flowers, path, sand, water, bridge, tree, bush, rock, stone wall and doorway. Every tile is opaque, because a room is one layer: trees, bushes and rocks are drawn standing on grass. Round shapes are stacked circles (dark, middle nudged up-left, small light spot), with the new `fillCircle` on the shared `PixelGrid`. As in game 02, a tile's index is its frame's position in the sheet, so new frames only go at the end. Solid: water, trees, bushes, rocks and walls.
  - **The camera covers only the room**, below the HUD strip, so every room is laid out from (0, 0) in its own coordinates. The HUD will get its own scene.
  - **H** in the world shows the solid tiles.
- **Art:** tiles, Wren, enemies, bosses and items are pixel maps with palettes, drawn with the shared `pixelMap` and `pixelSprites` helpers. No image files.
  - **Wren** (`src/content/sprites/wrenParts.ts`, `wren.ts`): an auburn ponytail, a teal tunic, an amber scarf and belt, and leather boots. Each 16×16 frame is stacked from a 7-row head, a 5-row torso and 4 rows of legs, so a face or a boot is drawn once. The front view faces down, the back view faces up, and the side view faces right. Left is right mirrored (with `mirrorPixelMap`, moved to `shared/` from game 04) into frames of its own, so a sprite never needs flipping and every animation is simply `wren-<name>-<facing>`.
  - **Animations:** idle and a two-step walk for each facing; a swing (wind up, thrust, recover) for each facing, whose length on screen the combat code will decide; item use, which is the thrust held; holding up a treasure; hurt (eyes squeezed shut, arms out; the flashing comes with combat); and defeat, two spins on the spot and then lying on her back.
  - **The sword** (`src/content/sprites/sword.ts`) is its own sprite with a frame per facing, not part of Wren's frames, so it can reach past her tile and be placed by the combat code.
  - **Sprite gallery:** `?scene=SpriteGallery` plays every animation. The gallery is now shared (`@shared/phaser/spriteGalleryScene`): it was game 02's, which uses it too, and a cell can show a single frame as well as an animation. Games 01 and 03 keep their own, which preview attacks and fighter poses.
- **Screen changes** (`src/systems/screenScroll.ts`): when Wren reaches an edge, player control stops, the old and new screens slide past together, and the new screen's enemies appear once the slide ends, as in the original.
  - **Areas** (`src/systems/worldMap.ts`, the world in `src/content/world/areas.ts`): the world is a set of areas, each a grid of screens. The overworld scrolls from screen to screen. A cave or house is an *interior*: one screen, entered through a doorway (`D`), and left by walking off its bottom edge, which puts Wren back one tile below that doorway, facing down. Each doorway in a map is listed in the room's `doors` with the screen and cell it leads to. Until the real Glimmervale exists, `testWorld.ts` has four screens in a 2×2 grid and a cave.
  - **The slide:** pushing against an edge that has a screen beyond it draws that screen's room one screen away, and the camera moves over to it in 0.7 s while Wren is carried across the edge by her body's size plus 4 px. Her body is off during the slide. Afterwards the new room, the camera and Wren all move back by one screen, so every room is still laid out from (0, 0). An edge with no screen beyond it is a wall.
  - **Doors** fade to black and back (0.25 s each way), and the room is swapped while the screen is dark.
  - **World check** (`src/systems/worldCheck.ts`), run at boot: every map is valid; neighbouring screens' shared edges match cell for cell (open against open, solid against solid), so an open edge always leads somewhere open; every doorway has exactly one door and every door is on a doorway; and every door leads to an open cell of a real screen, with an open cell below it to come back out on. Each error names the room and the cell.
  - **Tested** in the browser: from the north-west screen Wren walked right, down the bridge, left along the path and up again, round all four screens, landing 1–4 px inside each new screen; walked into the door and appeared in the cave at the door's target cell, facing up; walked off the cave's bottom and came out one tile below the door, facing down; and pushed against the west edge, which has no screen, without anything happening. The world check passed the test world, and named the room and cell for a mismatched edge, a doorway without a door, a door to a screen that does not exist and a door into a wall.
- **Movement:** eight directions with a fixed speed, facing the last direction pressed. Walking into the corner of a wall slides Wren around it when she is almost clear, so doorways are easy to hit. All numbers live in `config.ts` (`WREN`).
  - **The rules** (`src/systems/playerMovement.ts`) know nothing about Phaser: held directions become a velocity (80 px/s, the same diagonally), a facing and a corner slide. **Wren** (`src/entities/Wren.ts`) applies them to her physics body, a 10×6 box at her feet, and picks the idle or walk animation for her facing.
  - **Facing:** a direction pressed this frame wins if she is going that way. Otherwise she keeps her facing while she is still going that way, so walking diagonally does not flicker between two. Opposite directions held together cancel out.
  - **Corner slide:** walking straight (not diagonally) into something, if only one corner of her leading edge is blocked and she overlaps that tile by 6 px or less, she is moved sideways round it at walking speed.
  - **Found in testing:** her body's edges are rarely whole pixels. The first version tested the pixel at `right - 1`, so a body ending at 208.67 was taken to be clear of the tile starting at 208, while physics still stopped it: she slid onto the bridge from the right and got stuck 0.67 px short. The tests now use every pixel the body touches, its edges rounded outwards.
  - **Tested** in the browser by holding keys and reading her body from the running scene: a wall stopped her with her body's top at exactly 16 and the river with its bottom at 80; diagonally she moved at 56.57 px/s on each axis; right, then up as well, then letting go of up faced right, up, right; starting 2 px off the one-tile bridge on either side she slid on and crossed to the bottom of the screen, while 7 px off stopped her; walking into the lower corner of a rock slid her under it; and the gamepad stick moved her too.
- **Combat** (`src/systems/combat.ts`): hitboxes and hurtboxes are separate from physics bodies. A hit applies damage, knockback and a short invincibility flash. Enemy behaviour lives in small state machines in `src/systems/ai/`.
  - **The sword** (`src/entities/Sword.ts`) is its own sprite, held out beside Wren only while a thrust frame of her swing shows, so the swing animation alone decides its timing. Where it sits and what it reaches (blade and crossguard, up to 20 px from her middle) are set per facing in `config.ts` (`SWORD`). Facing up it is drawn behind her, so she hides the grip. A swing roots her to the spot.
  - **The spark** (`src/entities/Spark.ts`): a swing at full health also throws one, one at a time, flying over walls and water at 220 px/s until it hits an enemy or leaves the room.
  - **Fighting on a screen** (`src/systems/fight.ts`) owns the enemies (a physics group, so walls stop them and defeated ones leave it by themselves) and the spark, and each frame checks the sword and spark against the enemies and the enemies against Wren. It is cleared when the screen changes.
  - **Health** (`src/systems/gameState.ts`) is counted in half hearts, 3 hearts to start. A hit knocks Wren straight away from what hit her (along whichever axis they are further apart on, never diagonally) for 0.15 s without control, then she blinks and cannot be hurt for 1 s. At zero she spins twice and falls, and after a pause it is Game Over. Enemies are not solid: while she blinks she can walk through them.
  - **Enemies** flash red when hit and cannot be hit again for 0.25 s, longer than one thrust, so one swing hits once.
  - **The HUD** (`src/scenes/HudScene.ts`) runs beside the World scene over the strip at the top. The World publishes what it shows in the game's registry and the HUD redraws when it changes, so neither scene holds the other (see *HUD and pickups* below).
  - **Training dummy** (`src/entities/enemies/TrainingDummy.ts`): a straw test enemy that stands still, takes 3 hits and hurts on contact. Dev keys: **J** drops an enemy two tiles in front of Wren (the dummy, then each kind in turn), **K** takes half a heart from her.
  - **Tested** in the browser: at full health a swing threw a spark, and the dummy took one hit per swing (sword and spark together), was knocked 7 px back and flashed red, and was gone after three swings; below full health a swing threw no spark; K and walking into the dummy each took half a heart, the HUD showing the same numbers, and while she blinked, pushing on into the dummy did not hurt her again; the sword showed in all four facings; and at zero she spun, fell, and Game Over followed. A first screenshot of the downward swing showed no sword: headless Chrome ran so slowly that 110 ms after the press it was still on the wind-up frame. Logging her frame showed the sword out on every thrust frame.
- **Enemies** (`src/entities/enemies/`, numbers in `ENEMIES`): every kind extends `Enemy`, which holds health, contact damage, the red flash and knockback, and each frame calls the kind's own `think` with what it can sense (Wren's position, which cells are open, a random number source, and a way to spit pebbles). `think` is not called during a knockback.
  - **Blub**: wobbles for 0.5–1.1 s, then hops towards Wren for 0.38 s, in any of eight directions. 1 hit.
  - **Pebblenose**: walks from tile to tile (`src/systems/ai/gridWalk.ts`), only deciding at a tile's middle: usually straight on, sometimes a turn, back the way it came only when boxed in. At a tile it may stop for 0.5 s and spit a pebble halfway through. Knocked off the grid, it walks back to the middle of the tile it landed in. 2 hits.
  - **Pebbles** fly at 120 px/s and stop at anything solid or the room's edge. **Her shield** stops one flying straight at her face: she faces back along its path, walking or standing (a swing or a knockback leaves her open).
  - **Thornback**: plods along the grid; when Wren comes within 6 px of its row or column it charges straight at her at 150 px/s until something stops it, stands dazed for 0.6 s, and cannot charge again for 1.2 s. 3 hits, and a whole heart on contact.
  - **Flitter**: flies over walls and water (tiles never stop it, only the room's edges, which it bounces off), its heading swinging one way and then the other, and settles to rest now and then. 1 hit.
  - Pebblenose and Thornback are drawn from above facing down; their other facings are that drawing turned a quarter at a time, so each is drawn once.
  - **Spawning:** a room lists its enemies and the cell each starts in. They appear once the screen has finished sliding in (or while a door's fade is dark), and every visit starts them afresh. The world check refuses an enemy outside its room or on a solid cell, except flying ones.
  - **Drops** (`src/systems/drops.ts`, numbers in `DROPS`): a defeated enemy goes up in a puff and leaves a heart 15% of the time or a gem 40% of the time (worth 1, 5 or 20, most often 1). Pickups lie there 8 s, blinking for the last 2.5, and are collected by touching them: a heart gives back a heart, gems are counted.
  - **Tested** in the browser: on the start screen there were no enemies; walking east, none appeared mid-slide and all three once it ended; with Wren standing still both Blubs closed in on her in hops while the Pebblenose walked the grid; leaving and coming back put all three back on their starting cells; a pebble at her face was blocked when she faced it and took half a heart when she faced down; a Thornback in her row charged the length of the screen and was dazed at the trees; a Flitter crossed the river 16 times in 6 s without leaving the room; 20,000 drop rolls came out at 45.5% nothing, 14.7% hearts and 39.8% gems (31.8 / 7.2 / 0.8% by value); walking over a 5-gem and a heart added 5 gems and a heart; one swing at a Blub left a puff; and the world check refused a Blub in the river and a Thornback outside the room while allowing a Flitter over the water.
- **HUD and pickups** (layout and colours in `HUD`, limits in `CARRY`):
  - **The strip**, left to right: the **mini-map** (a small block per screen of the area, Wren's lit; inside a cave or house it shows the overworld with the screen she came in from lit), the **gem, key and bomb counters** (an icon and a zero-padded number each, so the row never shifts), a frame with the **item in hand**, and the **hearts** on the right. The World publishes one `HudData` object (`src/systems/hudData.ts`) whenever something in it changes and after every screen change.
  - **Limits:** 999 gems, 9 keys, and as many bombs as the bag holds: none before she finds it, 8 after.
  - **Pickups** can be a heart, a gem, a small key or bombs (4). Enemies drop hearts 15%, bombs 10% (only once she has the bag; before that this share leaves nothing, so the other odds stay the same) and gems 40% of the time; keys are never dropped.
  - **Placed pickups:** a room can list pickups that lie there from the start, such as a dungeon's small keys. They never run out, and once taken their id is kept in the game state so they do not come back. Bombs stay on the floor until she has the bag. The world check refuses two pickups with the same id and a pickup on a solid cell.
  - **Items** (`src/systems/items.ts`): the Moonrang, the bombs and the Lantern. The first item she finds goes into her hand; the bombs come with a full bag. Choosing the item in hand from the inventory comes with the items step.
  - **Dev keys:** **I** gives every item and a full bomb bag, **U** a key, **N** 50 gems (M is kept for muting), and **C** puts the next item she owns in her hand.
  - **Tested** in the browser: the counters started at 000 / 0 / 00 with an empty item box; walking over the red gem on the first screen gave 20 gems; the mini-map lit each of the four test screens in turn as she walked round them, and inside the cave still lit the screen with its door; the cave's key gave a key and was gone for good after leaving and coming back, while its bombs stayed on the floor until she had the bag, then took her from 2 to 6; I put the Moonrang in her hand and filled the bag to 8, and C went bombs, Lantern, Moonrang; 25 presses of the gem key and 12 of U stopped at 999 and 9; K twice showed 2 hearts; 20,000 drop rolls came out at 44.8% nothing, 14.8% hearts and 40.4% gems without the bag, and 35.3 / 15.0 / 10.1 / 39.6% (nothing, hearts, bombs, gems) with it; and the world check refused a repeated id and a key in a wall. Some first readings were off because the headless browser stalls for a moment after each screenshot, dropping a walk short and missing quick key taps; waiting after screenshots fixed the test.
- **Villagers, signs and the shop:**
  - **The dialogue box** (`src/scenes/DialogueScene.ts`, sizes in `DIALOGUE`) is laid over the frozen world like the pause menu: the speaker's name, then up to 3 lines typed out with the shared `Typewriter`. Z, Space, Enter or A shows the whole page, then the next; a blinking `>` means there is more. It sits at the bottom of the room, or at the top when Wren is in the lower half, so it never hides her. Text is written as one paragraph per page and word-wrapped at 48 characters (`src/systems/dialogue.ts`).
  - **Talking:** walk into someone or a signpost. People and wares are solid (a static physics group, `src/systems/talkers.ts`); the talk starts when she pushes against one she was not already against, or presses the direction afresh. For 0.4 s after a talk nothing starts one, so holding the key through the end of a talk (with the keyboard's repeats) does not start it again. The cooldown is timed with the game loop's clock: the scene's own clock still shows the moment the world froze when it resumes.
  - **People** (`src/content/people.ts`, sprites in `src/content/sprites/people.ts`): Gran Maudie, Pim the shopkeeper, Tolly the fisher and little Bree, each drawn from the front with a blink. Rooms place them by id; signs are the `S` tile, with their text in the room.
  - **The shop** (`src/systems/shop.ts`, prices in `SHOP`): wares stand with their price in the cell below, and are solid over both, so Wren stops where she can read it. Walking into one asks "Buy it?" with YES / NO (the shared `Menu`). A sale goes through only if she has the gems *and* a use for it: bombs need the bag and room in it, the potion needs a missing heart, and the Brightshield is sold once (its spot stays empty after). Otherwise Pim says why and no gems are taken. The Brightshield is kept in the game state; the projectiles only it can stop come with the dungeon enemies.
  - **The world check** also refuses a signpost without exactly one text, people or wares on solid cells, a price over a solid cell, and any text that does not fit a page or uses a character the pixel font cannot draw (checked for every person and every shop question, used or not).
  - **Test world:** Gran, Bree and a sign on the first screen, and Pim's shop (with Tolly visiting) behind a new door on the north-east screen. Emberfen itself comes with the overworld step.
  - **Tested** in the browser: walking into Bree, Gran, the sign, Pim and Tolly each paused the world and showed every page in full, with the box at the bottom while Wren was in the upper half; the world ran again afterwards. Holding left with key repeats through the end of Bree's talk did not start it again for 2 s, while letting go and pressing again did. The shop rules were checked straight against `buy()` (bombs with no bag, a full bag, 10 and 20 gems; the potion at full health, with 39 and 40 gems; the shield with 89, 90 and after it was sold: each bought only when it should, and gems changed only then), and through the box: YES on bombs with no bag took nothing, NO on the potion took nothing, YES took 40 gems and refilled her hearts (HUD too), the shield with 60 gems was refused with "Come back with 30 more", and with 200 it was bought and taken off show, still gone after leaving and coming back. The world check refused a sign text too long for a page, a `~`, a signpost with no text and Gran in a tree.
  - Three problems found while testing: the talk cooldown used the scene's clock, which is stale at the moment the world resumes, so it had already run out and a held key started the talk again; a fresh press straight into a *different* talker within the cooldown was ignored for good, so the game now remembers which one she was against; and walking up to a ware put Wren's head over it and her feet on its price, so wares are now solid over their price too.
- **Glimmervale** (`src/content/world/overworld.ts`, `interiors.ts`): the test world is gone; this is the real one.
  - **The overworld** is 5 × 4 screens with Emberfen in the middle: Deep Wood, Mossroot Hollow, Old Pass, Spire Gate and Frost Ridge along the top; Wood Edge, Forest Path, North Road, Stone Hills and Hermit's Rise; West Meadow, Emberfen West, Emberfen, Emberfen East and the Eastern Hills; and Reed Shore, the Sunken Lake, Lake Bank, the Southern Fields and the Cliff Coast along the bottom. Neighbours always meet through a two-tile gap (rows 4 and 5 on an east or west edge, columns 9 and 10 on a north or south edge), so edges match by construction; Forest Path and Emberfen West, and Spire Gate and the Stone Hills, are not joined.
  - **Places:** Gran's house and Pim's shop in Emberfen (Bree outside, Tolly fishing at Lake Bank); the Mossroot Cellar's door in a ruined wall in Mossroot Hollow and the Sunken Vault's on an island in the lake, each leading for now to a hall with a sign, until the dungeons exist; the Crystal Spire at Spire Gate, its door sealed by crystal; and Old Fennick's cave on Hermit's Rise. Villages have no enemies; the forest has Blubs and Flitters, the hills Thornbacks and Pebblenoses, the lake Flitters.
  - **New tiles:** roof, house wall, house door, wooden floor, stump, cliff, cave mouth, cracked cliff and crystal (added at the end of the sheet, so older tile indices never change).
  - **Secrets:** two **cracked cliffs** (Frost Ridge, Cliff Coast) are *secret walls*: a bomb opens one into a cave mouth, which is then a door like any other and stays open (its id is kept in the game state; the room is drawn with it open on every visit). Each cave holds a **heart container** (a new pickup: one more heart, and all hearts filled); the Cliff Coast cave also has two red gems. Until bombs exist, dev key **B** opens a cracked cliff just in front of Wren. Three **hidden gems** wait by stumps (Deep Wood, Reed Shore, the Southern Fields): striking the stump with the sword brings the gem out in a puff, onto Wren's side of the stump so she can reach it. Tolly and Bree drop hints.
  - **The Lantern:** Old Fennick gives it the first time Wren talks to him (a person can have a *gift*, handed over as the dialogue box closes), then says something else. The first item she gets goes into her hand.
  - **Everything connects:** at boot, after the other checks, the world check walks the whole world from the start the way Wren could (to neighbouring cells, across screen edges, through doors, out of the bottom of caves and houses back to their door, and through every cracked cliff as if bombed) and refuses to start if any screen, pickup, hidden spot, person, sign, ware or secret wall cannot be reached, naming each one (`src/systems/reachability.ts`).
  - **Tested** in the browser: the world check passed with all 27 screens (20 outside, 7 inside) reached, and refused a gem walled in by trees and a hermit's cave whose mouth was bricked up. The whole overworld was drawn into one picture to look it over. Walking north twice from Emberfen, back, west and south over the bridge changed screens each time and moved the mini-map; the Vault's door led in and back out below it; B turned Frost Ridge's cracked cliff into an open cave mouth, which was still open after leaving the cave and after leaving the screen and coming back; the two heart containers took her from 3 to 4 to 5 hearts (full each time) and the Cliff Coast cave's gems added 40; the three stumps gave 20, 20 and 5 gems; Old Fennick gave the Lantern once, which went into her hand, and the second time only talked; and Emberfen's two doors led to Gran and to Pim's counter.
- **Dungeon rooms and doors:**
  - **Dungeons** are a third kind of area: a grid of rooms that scroll like the overworld. A room is written as its 18 × 8 inside only; `dungeonRoom()` (`src/content/world/dungeonRoom.ts`) builds the wall a tile thick round it, with a two-tile doorway on each side that has a door. The map shows every doorway as floor; doors that are closed right now are drawn over it when the room is shown, and taken off (in a puff) when they open. New tiles: dungeon wall, floor and block, and the closed looks of the four door kinds.
  - **Side doors** (`src/systems/dungeonDoors.ts`), listed per side by both rooms: **open**; **locked**, which Wren opens by walking into it with a small key (the key is spent); **shut**, closed while the room she is in still has enemies, so it shuts behind her and opens once they are all defeated; **cracked**, a wall until a bomb opens it (for now dev key **B**); and the **boss** door, which the dungeon's boss key opens (the key is kept). A door is the same door from both rooms (its id is named after the room above or to the left of it), so opening it on one side opens it on the other. Opened doors, blasted walls and cleared rooms are kept in the game state: a cleared dungeon room's enemies do not come back.
  - **Sliding into a dungeon room** carries Wren a tile further than outdoors, past the wall, so a shut door never closes on her. Walking out of the entrance room's bottom doorway goes back out of the door that led in.
  - **Map, compass and boss key:** new pickups, each for the dungeon it lies in. The HUD's mini-map shows a dungeon's rooms as Wren visits them, all of them once she has the map, and the boss room in red once she has the compass. (The inventory will show them too.)
  - **The world check** also requires, in dungeons: doorways open exactly where a room lists a door, the same door kind listed on both sides, only a bottom door leading out (the entrance), enemies on at least one side of a shut door, every map, compass and boss key in its own dungeon, and a real boss room. The reachability walk goes out through the entrance too.
  - **The test dungeon** (`src/content/world/testDungeon.ts`), 3 × 3 rooms behind the Mossroot Hollow door, needs every kind of door: the entrance holds the key for the locked door up to the hub; the cracked wall to its right hides the second key, for the hub's locked left door and the boss key behind it; the room right of the hub shuts behind Wren and holds the compass; the map is left of the entrance; and the boss door leads to an empty boss room with a sign. The real Mossroot Cellar replaces it with dungeon 1.
  - **Tested** in the browser, walking it end to end: the Mossroot door led into the entrance, with only that room on the mini-map; walking into the locked door with no key did nothing, and with the key from the floor opened it (keys 1 → 0) and carried her into the hub clear of the wall, the door showing open from that side too; the hub's shut door was open (no enemies there), and in the next room it shut behind her with 2 enemies inside and would not let her back; defeating them opened it and marked the room cleared, and on coming back no enemies returned; the compass marked the boss room; B opened the cracked wall for good and the second key opened the hub's left door; the map showed all 7 rooms; the boss key opened the boss door and was kept; and walking out of the entrance came back out in Mossroot Hollow. The world check refused a door listed as open on one side and locked on the other, a shut door with no enemies either side, and a map belonging to another dungeon.
- **Items** (`src/systems/itemUse.ts`, numbers in `MOONRANG`, `BOMB` and `LANTERN`): **X** (or B on a gamepad) uses the item in hand; she holds it out for a moment, rooted to the spot.
  - **The Moonrang** (`src/entities/Moonrang.ts`) spins out the way she faces for up to 5 tiles, then home to her. Anything solid, the room's edge or an enemy turns it back early; an enemy it strikes is **stunned** for 2 s (frozen, tinted blue, its animation paused; it can still be hit, and still hurts to touch). Gems, hearts and keys it touches ride back with it and are collected when it reaches her; heart containers, bombs and dungeon treasures stay put. One at a time.
  - **Bombs** (`src/entities/Bomb.ts`): one is set down in front of her from her bag, blinks faster and faster, and after 1.5 s blows up. Everything whose middle is within 26 px is caught: enemies take 2 hits, Wren loses a heart if she stayed, and every cracked cliff or cracked dungeon wall within reach opens. Two at most on screen. The **B** dev key that stood in for them is gone.
  - **The Lantern** holds out a flickering flame. A room can be **dark** (drawn under a near-black shadow); the flame lights it, the shadow fading away, for the rest of the visit. The flame also reports where it burns, for the torches of the next step.
  - **The inventory** (`src/scenes/InventoryScene.ts`) shows the items she owns in a row, in a fixed order; left and right move the cursor, and the item under it is in her hand straight away (and in the HUD). It shows the item's name and her bombs, and Enter or Esc closes it. With nothing yet it says so.
  - A room of the test dungeon (the key room) is dark.
  - **A freeze found on the way:** the Lantern's flame is drawn with a line to a point between pixels, and the shared `PixelGrid.line` (Bresenham) steps a whole pixel at a time, so it could never land on that point and looped forever: the game froze while drawing its sprites at boot. The shared line now rounds both ends to whole pixels first (a no-op for every line the other games draw).
  - **Tested** in the browser: the inventory said NO ITEMS YET, then after I listed the Moonrang, bombs and Lantern with BOMBS 8 / 8, and right, right, right, left went bombs, Lantern, Moonrang, Lantern, the HUD following. The Moonrang thrown at a Blub struck it 70 px out, came home and was caught; the Blub stood frozen at the same spot for 2 s, then moved on. Along a row of pickups it brought back a heart, a 5-gem and a key and left the heart container lying. Thrown at the room's edge it turned back there. Bombs: each press took one from the bag (8 → 7), a third press with two down did nothing, the two blasts defeated a 3-hit Thornback and took one heart from Wren beside them (only one, as she then blinks); a bomb opened Frost Ridge's cracked cliff and the test dungeon's cracked wall. The key room was dark (0.88), the Lantern lifted it, and after going to the hub and back it was dark again.
- **Game state** (`src/systems/gameState.ts`): hearts, gems, keys, bombs, items, the item in hand, collected pickups, the Brightshield, opened secret walls and doors, cleared and visited dungeon rooms, each dungeon's map, compass and boss key, and later opened doors and defeated bosses, in one plain object, so it can be saved and loaded.
- **Saving:** one save slot in `localStorage`, with a version number. A save is read as untrusted data and checked field by field before it is used; a broken or unknown save is ignored. Storage can be missing (private windows), so every read and write is wrapped and the game still runs without it.
- **Text:** all in-game text uses `addPixelText`, and dialogue uses the shared typewriter.
- **Sound:** sword, hits, pickups, doors, bombs, secrets and one loop each for the overworld, the dungeons and the bosses, using the shared Web Audio engine.
- **Scenes** (`src/scenes/`): Boot → Title (or Controls) → World → Game Over / Ending. The pause menu (the shared one) and the inventory are laid over a frozen World rather than replacing it. On a gamepad, Start opens the inventory and Select pauses.
  - **Keys let go of during a pause.** A paused scene does not see key events, so a key released while the pause menu or inventory was open would still count as held once the World resumes. The World resets its keys whenever it resumes.
- **Dev tools:** `?scene=` starts any scene straight away (not Pause or Inventory, which need a World under them), a sprite gallery shows every animation, and dev keys in the world (keyboard only) give items, gems and keys (I, U, N), cycle the item in hand (C), drop enemies (J), hurt Wren (K) and show physics bodies (H). Until real defeats and the real ending exist, **G** goes to Game Over and **V** to the ending.
